# Setting Canon — Vanguard Contested   (ground truth; overrides recollection)
*A condensed digest for play orientation. The deep canon lives in `references/`; this file points there. Casualty figures are scenario illustrations — ranges, not predictions.*

## Premise / tone
A mid-October Tuesday, ~14:00 UTC: the **Pulse**. One in five humans manifests a power; animals begin turning to monsters (most new biomass marine); rifts open; an **EMP** kills unshielded electronics worldwide. Within one week ~11 billion humanoid **crossers** arrive — eighteen refugee-nations fleeing a worse threat on their side. Seventeen are internally divided peoples (a bloc's stance is the *average* of its members); the eighteenth is the unified, aggressive **Empire**. Humanity is beaten in the open, but a fighting alliance holds and a defended **redoubt** survives: organized defeat, with somewhere to stand.

This is a lethal, gritty, powered post-apocalypse. **M&M 3e, PL 8–9. ~80% mortality; death is permanent and the default.** Tone is history-book-into-crime-novel: plain, declarative, concrete; scale through consequence, never gore. The horror is systemic — the dark grid, the ration line, the refugee column, the funeral with no priest — not splatter. **Crossers are peoples, not monsters; even enemies have reasons.** Nuclear forces reach alert and signaling only; no detonation. Every figure is fictional.

## Places (regions, danger bands, lethality numbers)
Three danger bands: **Guarded** (redoubt) · **Embattled** (contested) · **Hostile-Held / No-Man's-Land** (overrun). Tune encounters to the region's band, not the PC's confidence.

**The default sandbox is the North American redoubt frontier.** The campaign opens in **Manhattan at ~10:00**, runs the corridor's fall over the first ~90 days, and bends west to the Interior North muster-town. The road *is* the campaign.

Frontier sites, west-bound:
- **Day-Zero Manhattan** — No-Man's-Land forming · ~18% · Tension floor 5 (opening scene 6). The grid is gone, one in five just changed, the island has an afternoon before it goes dark. No crosser blocs yet — only collapsing institutions (precincts, hospitals, the Guard, the crossings, crews, mutual-aid blocks, private security). The crossings (GW, Lincoln, Holland, tunnels) jam with dead cars and control the exodus; medical centers (Bellevue, NYP/Mount Sinai, the VA) are the most fought-over sites.
- **The Mid-Atlantic Corridor** (NYC outer boroughs–Jersey–Philadelphia–Baltimore–DC) — No-Man's-Land · ~18% · Tension floor 6. The contested belt being given up; governance relocated inland. Holdout enclaves, scavenger crews, the desperate who stayed, and **Reaver raid-lines** from the north. The frontier's lethal edge and richest ground — most early characters die here.
- **The Road West** — five engine-rolled legs, lethality dropping inland, the Authority's grip rising as it falls: (1) a tower-fief over a chokepoint (No-Man's-Land); (2) a refugee camp that turned (No-Man's-Land); (3) a stronghold that's too quiet — press-gang/cult behind a clean face (Embattled); (4) a stripped river crossing, wrong currency (Embattled); (5) a water-treatment plant that's a moral trap — clean water and a conscription line in the same door (Guarded), the threshold of Marrow Creek.
- **Marrow Creek** — the Interior North muster-town · Guarded · ~8% · Tension floor 4. The home base: a reconstituted town under emergency authority, the forward muster-camp facing the corridor, the safest the survivor will be all year (not very). The **Redoubt Authority** garrisons it with a **Zealot** streak — ideological about registration and discipline. A Compact enclave-town sits a few miles off. Heal, resupply (against the Supply track, never free), court factions, take Authority missions. Unsecured edges: the Quarry Lair (denned feral), the Rigged Field Hospital (booby-trapped), the Empty Depot (stripped bare).

Wider map (named lethality numbers, preserved):
- **Redoubt / Guarded:** NA Interior North ~8% (*safest large zone on Earth*); Interior South / Mountain West / Canada Core ~10–11%; Northern Europe ~13%; South Asia / India ~15% (held but bloodied, the largest population anchor).
- **Contested / Embattled:** Western Europe ~16%; Mexico & Central America ~17%; South America South ~17%; NA Southeast (in places) ~16–18%.
- **Overrun / No-Man's-Land:** Mid-Atlantic Corridor ~18% · New England ~18% (Reavers; half-emptied) · NA Pacific Coast ~16–18% · Central Asia–Siberia ~16% (*Empire heartland*) · Russia ~16% · Middle East ~18% (Warrior-Kingdom) · East Asia/China ~16% · Japan & Korea ~19% (encircled) · Southeast Asia ~18% · North Africa–Sahel ~18% · West Africa/Lagos ~19% · Central/East/Southern Africa ~18% · Oceania ~19% · South America North ~17%.

**Travel:** powered long-range transport is dead (EMP); oceanic shipping is permanently severed (marine monsters). Movement is foot, horse, sail, salvaged pre-electronic vehicles, and — for the rare flyer — the air. The inter-regional network stays broken all year; the road is a hazard in itself.

## Factions (who wants what — brief; full detail in references/04 and references/13)
Seventeen of eighteen crosser blocs are internally divided — **diplomacy is sub-faction** (turn a war-band, a chief, a house — not "the Reavers"). Only the Empire acts as one will.

The four powers that touch the starting frontier:
- **The Redoubt Authority** (Tier 4, the PC's likely home) — the emergency government of the interior line: military remnants, organized empowered, the Compact alliance. Registers, conscripts, rations, holds the wall. *At the sandbox it is Marrow Creek.* Tension: autonomy vs. duty; the fairness of the empowered-class system.
- **The Interior Compact** (Bloc 3, Tier 5, +2 allied, unitary) — principled crosser refuge-seekers; **the reason the NA redoubt holds.** Mixed human-Compact settlements; alien capability on humanity's side. Favor is conditional on just governance.
- **The Reavers** (Bloc 9, Tier 3, −2/−3, divided) — decentralized New England warbands pressing south; no throne to treat with, each war-chief a faction. Crossers who believe they are also refugees owed land — therefore *dealable*, band by band.
- **The Atlantic Concord** (Bloc 17, Tier 5, +1 wavering toward 0, unitary) — the advanced North-Atlantic people, **humanity's crown-jewel potential ally**, hedging toward neutral while humanity looks doomed. Their hesitation is why the Mid-Atlantic falls. **Winning them fully is a campaign-scale objective**; they judge by what they can see.

Beyond the frontier: **the Empire** (Bloc 1, Tier 6, war) — the lone unified conqueror, ~25% of crosser power, seated in Central Asia–Siberia; the pole the other hostiles orbit (Warrior-Kingdom, Corsair Order, Pacific Houses, Reavers). Loyal anchor allies: the European trade-confederation (Bloc 11), the pilgrim-nation (Bloc 15, India), the East-Asian monastic order (Bloc 7). The **Feral Swarm** (Tier 5, no will) is the universal ambient threat; sapient **monster-lords** break districts. *Full faction list, clocks, and generation procedure: references/04. Runnable frontier dossiers with peelable sub-factions and track levers: references/13.*

## Timeline / history (the one-year arc)
1. **The Day (hours).** Pulse and EMP together; aviation, critical care, traffic take catastrophic losses; the world goes dark in an afternoon. No one understands what happened.
2. **The First Two Weeks.** Rifts disgorge. The Empire lands its land vanguard across Central Asia–Siberia and moves with purpose while everyone is blind; the Reavers come through into New England; the Concord hesitates.
3. **The First 90 Days.** The hostile coalition coheres around the Empire faster than fractured humanity can answer. The Mid-Atlantic corridor is given up; the season's great civilian event is the **evacuation west**. The interior line forms; Northern Europe and India harden around their loyal allies.
4. **The First Year.** The redoubt stabilizes (~2B behind a secure line); the Empire becomes the ascendant pole on Earth; the off-screen broader threat remains unanswered.

The four driving questions, answered: **New York** evacuated and largely lost as a unified city, governance inland, holdouts remain; **the Euro** collapses (markets froze at the EMP, barter and scrip fill the gap); **the interior child** lives a guarded-redoubt childhood (ration lines, wood-stove winters, shelter drills — scarcity, not graphic peril); **the conflict map** is Empire-ascendant with humanity holding a shrinking but real perimeter beside its loyal blocs.

**The long horizon — the Unmaking.** The crossers fled something: a slow, world-consuming force or predator-power that renders planes uninhabitable behind it. Its clock is **slow (measured in years)**, so in Year One it is rumor and dread — the reason crossers are sympathetic, not an active force. It is the campaign's true endgame; whether the rifts close, stay open, or become a door it can follow is the central long-arc mystery.

## Named NPCs (name — role — wants)
*Warlord+ figures are above PL 8–9 — flee, deal, or out-think; never duel. Voices are binding once spoken. Stat references and full dossiers: references/12.*

The Empire:
- **The Iron Regent** — Crown (~300×), the Empire's field commander in the West — wants the line to advance on schedule; a clock and a horizon, not an encounter. *"The line moves on schedule. Sentiment is not a variable."*
- **Vurn of the Seventh Spear** — Warlord vanguard officer — wants a breakthrough to report; the redoubt's likely first named Empire foe.

The Reavers (New England; peelable):
- **Hesk Gravewater** — Warlord, paramount-ish chief holding the Connecticut approaches — wants ground for his people; *dealable* (he believes the Reavers are refugees owed land). *"Everyone's running from something, friend. We just stopped."*
- **The Knot-Sisters** — two paired Heavies (PL 8), the highest-value early peel — want to stop dying for nothing; turn them and a war-band leaves the Tide.
- **Yorl Ashwidow** — drags a refugee-mass, transactional, the most peelable band. **Drevel Coldmarrow** — fast nomadic raiding column, can't be pinned (deal or evade). **Varn Saltbroke** — mass-dragging and openly hostile, the one you flee.

The redoubt & its allies:
- **The Marshal of the Interior Line** — runs Marrow Creek and the conscription system (unpowered, or a Heavy) — wants the line held and every body classed; the PC's likely commander, the face of autonomy-vs-duty. *"I don't need you to like the order. I need you on the wall when it comes."*
- **Sael-of-the-Open-Hand** — Compact Titan envoy, the ally who makes the redoubt viable — wants to understand who humanity *is* before committing the Compact's full weight; a moral mirror with strategic teeth. *"Show me how you decide who eats. Then I will know who you are."*
- **First Voice of the Concord** — Crown, the wavering elite ally's representative — wants proof humanity can hold; moved by demonstrated resolve, not pleas. *"We have buried one world. We are careful which graves we dig beside."*

Humanity, in the wild:
- **The Breaker of Pittsburgh** — Siege (above-PL sub-boss), a strongman who carved a fief from a corridor city — wants his fief fed and unconquered; the moral-gray power the PC measures against. *"My people eat tonight. Yours can too, if you're mine by morning."*
- **Doc** — Heavy (PL 7–8), a wandering healer-protector of the evacuation roads — wants only to keep the road's wounded alive; the natural recurring ally. *"I can't fix the world. I can fix you. Hold still."*

Monster-lord:
- **The Drowning Crown** — Horror-tier marine kaiju that closed the North Atlantic — the reason the sea off New England is death; a force of nature, never an encounter.

Starting seed cast (engine-rolled; bind PC-tied slots at character creation — references/12):
- **Imani Voss** — corridor fixer/broker — needs the PC, guards a secret, has already betrayed someone.
- **Dev Cho** — from the PC's old life *[bind at creation]* — seeks redemption, hiding that they manifested too.
- **Mira Larkin** — mechanic who keeps the salvaged vehicles running *[bind at creation]* — wants out west; tied to the PC's past.
- **Oziel Voss** — medic (Imani's sibling) — wants belonging; hiding someone behind the supply curtain.
- **Cyrus Okafor** — refugee parent — desperate for food/meds, afraid, marked by an enemy (help him, inherit his hunters).
- **Nadia Mwangi** — empowered free agent — curious, wants a way out, quietly a crosser sympathizer/back-channel.

## Hard content lines (tone discipline)
- **Crossers are peoples, not monsters.** Even enemies have reasons; render their cause, never caricature it.
- **Systemic horror, not gore.** Scale through consequence (the dark grid, the ration line, the refugee column, the funeral with no priest), never graphic splatter. The interior-child lens stays slice-of-life.
- **No real named individuals** in public roles (name by office); no operational/weapons detail. Nuclear forces reach alert and signaling only — never detonation. Every figure is fictional.
- **Two banned phrases:** never use **"load-bearing"** or **"one thing I want to name."** They break the table's voice.
