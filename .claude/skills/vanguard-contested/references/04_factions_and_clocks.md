# 04 · FACTIONS, CLOCKS & FACTION GENERATION
*The complete faction reference across the year: who acts at Day Zero, how to run factions, how to generate new ones as the map changes, and the mature factions the year produces. Factions carry **Tier (1–6)**, **Hold (Strong/Weak)**, a **relationship track** toward the PC (−3 war … +3 sworn), and a **clock** (the project they advance offscreen).*

> Reminder: seventeen of eighteen crosser blocs are internally divided — a bloc's stance is the *average* of its members, so even hostile blocs hold peelable friendly minorities. Only the Empire acts as one will. **Diplomacy is sub-faction.**

---

## THE DAY-ZERO LANDSCAPE (before the named factions exist)
At Day Zero the crossers have not arrived (week one) and the redoubt does not exist (~90 days out). What acts is the **collapse of standing human institutions** under the Pulse and EMP, plus the **newly-manifested empowered** (1 in 5). Everything is blind, dark, and local. These are the powers in play in the opening act.

### In New York City
- **NYPD — fracturing into precinct fiefs.** No citywide comms, no central command; armed and organized at the station-house level. Some precincts hold order and run curfews; some desert; some predate. The most immediately powerful armed institution on most blocks. *Goal: hold their ground. Tier 3, Hold weak (fragmented).*
- **Hospitals & EMS — besieged triage islands.** The EMP kills life support, monitors, and ambulances at the worst possible moment; mass casualties from the pulse (surgeries, aircraft, traffic). The big medical centers (Bellevue, the NYP and Mount Sinai campuses, the VA) become the most valuable and most desperate sites in the city — everyone wants in. *Goal: keep people alive with nothing. Tier 2, the prize everyone fights over.*
- **NY National Guard & military — slow, hardened, decisive if it coheres.** Armories are strongpoints; units are EMP-degraded but disciplined. Whoever gets the Guard organized owns the evacuation, the crossings, and the future. *The nucleus of the eventual Redoubt Authority. Tier 4 if it coheres, Tier 2 if it doesn't.*
- **Port Authority & the crossings — chokepoint power.** Bridges and tunnels jam instantly with dead vehicles; the subway is a dark death-trap. Control of the crossings is control of who gets off the island. *Whoever holds the George Washington / the Lincoln / the tunnels controls the exodus.*
- **Organized crime & street crews — adapting fastest.** The mob, established gangs, and new opportunists seize the logistics of scarcity: food, fuel, the rare working generator, protection. Lawlessness is their native element. *Goal: own the black market. Tier 2–3, Hold strong locally.*
- **Mutual-aid micro-institutions — the real survival layer.** Churches, mosques, synagogues, community boards, union locals, building supers, bodega networks. The blocks that live are the ones with a functioning micro-institution. *The seed of every future holdout enclave.*
- **The newly-empowered (~1.7M in the metro) — the wild card.** Mostly Flickers and Sparks; a scattering of Bruisers and Heavies, a rare Breaker. Unorganized — individuals choosing in the first hours to protect, prey, flee, or be recruited by whoever is organized. They reshuffle every other faction. *The PC is one of these.*
- **Private security & hoarded wealth — fortified islands.** Helicopters won't fly (EMP); the wealthy can't leave fast, so private security and stockpiles turn corporate campuses and towers into walled redoubts-in-miniature.
- **The information vacuum.** Media goes dark; rumor, the few shielded radios, and runners become a force in themselves. Panic is governed by whoever controls what people believe is happening.

### Across America
- **The federal government — blind and testing whether it coheres.** Continuity-of-government protocols exist, but the EMP severs command. Whether Washington reconstitutes (toward the future Redoubt Authority) or fragments is the great national question of the first 90 days.
- **The US military / NORTHCOM — the most survivable large institution.** Hardened comms, fuel, discipline; EMP-degraded but functional. Bases become strongpoints; the interior installations are the skeleton the redoubt grows on. Nuclear forces go to alert and signaling only — never used.
- **State & regional authorities — the fast-coherers.** Governors with Guard units become regional powers. The **interior states cohere faster than the coasts** — this is, geographically, why the redoubt forms where it does.
- **FEMA & emergency management — the evacuation framework**, instantly overwhelmed, but the scaffolding the redoubt path runs on.
- **Law enforcement — local and increasingly autonomous.** Sheriffs and county forces become the government in much of the country.
- **Organized crime & cartels — continental black-market logistics**, adapting fast across borders.
- **Armed civilians, preppers & militias — the great American wild card.** A heavily armed populace: some become the backbone of holdout defense, some become warlords. Rural counties self-organize overnight.
- **Corporations, industry & infrastructure — physical, not financial.** Megacorps-as-sovereigns do not happen: the EMP kills the networks, finance, and logistics their power rested on, and the severed sea ends global supply. Brands, markets, IP, and most tech/finance/retail giants evaporate. What survives is *physical plant*, seized locally — refineries and fuel depots, grain elevators and food warehouses, pharma and ammunition stock, water plants, arms factories, islanded power generation, the rare hardened comms/compute node. These are among the most fought-over resources on the continent, run by whoever is standing on them, and are progressively requisitioned by the cohering authorities. The redoubt's industrial base *is* the carcass of the corporate economy under new ownership. The one corporate function that becomes raw power is **private security / PMCs** — armed men who detach from an employer who can't pay or reach them and become mercenary bands, enclave guards, or warlord muscle.
- **Religious & civic networks — the national mutual-aid backbone.**
- **The empowered, nationwide (~65M; ~2–3M Breaker+).** Scattered, unorganized, and reshuffling every power structure they touch.

### The transition (week 1 onward)
Then the crossers arrive and the named factions begin to crystallize on top of this: the Empire's vanguard lands in Eurasia (far off, but the gravity well of the war); rifts near North America disgorge; the **Reavers** head into New England; the **Interior Compact** makes contact with the cohering interior authorities. The Day-Zero institutions don't vanish — they become the raw material the mature factions are built from (the Guard + interior states + Compact → the Redoubt Authority; predatory crews and empowered warlords → the lost-zone powers; mutual-aid enclaves → the holdouts).

## RUNNING FACTIONS
- **Each faction has a goal, assets, Tier/Hold, a relationship track, and a clock.** That is enough to run it.
- **They act offscreen, from their own goals and knowledge** — never from narrative convenience. Advance their clocks at session end and whenever the PC wastes time. World events proceed from their causes whether the PC is present or not.
- **Show the PC only the clocks they could perceive.** A distant clock (Empire consolidation) is rumor; a local one (the Reaver Tide, the precinct annexing your block) is visible pressure.
- **Factions remember and react.** The relationship track shifts with the PC's actions; a faction wronged adapts and retaliates; a faction helped opens doors and asks for more.
- **Divided blocs negotiate sub-faction.** You turn a war-band, a chief, a single house — not "the Reavers." Unitary powers (Empire, Concord, Compact) treat as wholes.
- **The landscape is not static.** Factions collapse, merge, get absorbed, or transform when their clock fails or a rival wins (see generation, below). Prune dead factions and grow new ones so the map stays alive.

## GENERATING NEW FACTIONS (as the world changes)
New factions emerge constantly in the first year. Generate one whenever there is a **trigger**: a power vacuum (an institution collapses), a **crosser arrival or bloc split**, a region's **status changes** (the corridor falls → warlords and enclaves bloom), a **clock completes**, or the **PC's own actions** create or shatter a group. Build it with dice — don't take the first idea.

**Procedure (roll with code):**
1. **Name the trigger** — what changed, and what vacuum or pressure it created.
2. **Roll origin (d8):**
   1. *Splintered institution* — an ex-precinct, Guard unit, sheriff's department, hospital security force gone independent.
   2. *Criminal expansion* — a gang, crew, or cartel arm that seized the logistics of scarcity.
   3. *Empowered power* — a Breaker+ who gathered followers (roll: protector / tyrant / cult).
   4. *Grown enclave* — a mutual-aid network, congregation, or fortified neighborhood become a polity.
   5. *Armed bloc* — preppers, a militia, a rural county that organized.
   6. *Crosser sub-faction* — a war-band, clan, house, or refugee group peeled off a bloc and localized.
   7. *Industrial/infrastructure remnant* — a site-based group (refinery, arms plant, grid or water operator, distribution hub) run by its former workforce and security; the carcass of a corporation, valuable for what it physically controls.
   8. *Mercenary company* — a detached PMC or corporate security force gone independent, selling organized force to whoever pays in food, fuel, or fealty.
3. **Roll drive (d6):**
   1. *Survival / hoarding* — control the supply of food, fuel, water.
   2. *Order / control* — impose law and a monopoly on force over territory.
   3. *Predation* — raid, extort, take; grow by force.
   4. *Sanctuary* — protect a population; build a safe place.
   5. *Restoration* — retake lost ground or rebuild a fallen institution / the old world.
   6. *Ideology* — spread a creed, recruit the empowered, remake society.
4. **Assign Tier (1–6) + Hold** from assets and cohesion. Emergent factions usually start **Tier 1–3**. Strong = unified command, a Breaker+ leader, or a real strongpoint; Weak = loose, divided, or leaderless.
5. **Seed the relationship** to the PC and to neighbors (default neutral-wary in the chaos; roll if unsure).
6. **Give them one clock** (4–8 segments) — the project they now advance offscreen ("the precinct annexes the next ten blocks: 6"; "the warlord absorbs the rival crews: 4").
7. **Place them and note assets** — territory, numbers, the one or two things they control that matter.

**Factions also die.** When a clock fails or a rival wins, resolve the loser: *absorbed* (folds into the victor), *scattered* (dissolves into refugees and free agents), or *mutated* (re-roll origin/drive — the remnant becomes something new). Roll if unclear. The map should look meaningfully different by Day +90, +180, +365.

## THE MATURE FACTIONS (week one onward — what the year produces)

### Crosser factions
- **The Empire (Bloc 1) — Tier 6, Strong · −3 (war).** The lone unified conqueror; ~25% of crosser power; seat in the Central Asia–Siberia heartland. The singular antagonist and the pole the other hostiles orbit. Methods: organized conquest, vanguard spearheads, vassalizing weaker blocs.
- **The Reavers (Bloc 9) — Tier 3, Weak but fierce · −2/−3.** Decentralized high-per-capita warbands in New England; no throne to treat with. Each war-chief is a faction. Methods: raiding, terror, attrition on the Mid-Atlantic.
- **The Warrior-Kingdom (Bloc 5) — Tier 4, Strong · −2.** A martial feudal crosser-state holding the Middle East; the southern hostile arc.
- **The Corsair Order (Bloc 13) — Tier 3, Strong · −2.** A disciplined naval order; Pacific-maritime; ties into the sea collapse and the encirclement of East Asia.
- **The Pacific Houses (Bloc 16) — Tier 5, Weak (split) · −1.** A mighty culture of feuding great houses, ELITE per-capita; some join the Empire, some hold out. A live diplomatic prize, house by house.
- **The Atlantic Concord (Bloc 17) — Tier 5, Strong · +1 (wavering toward 0).** The advanced North-Atlantic people; ELITE; humanity's crown-jewel potential ally — hedging to near-neutral, which is why the Mid-Atlantic falls. **Winning them fully is a campaign-scale objective.**
- **The Interior Compact (Bloc 3) — Tier 5, Strong · +2 (allied).** The principled refuge-seekers anchoring the North American interior. The reason the NA redoubt holds; mixed human-Compact settlements.
- **Loyal anchor allies — Tier 4–5 · +2.** The European trade-confederation (Bloc 11, Northern Europe), the pilgrim-nation (Bloc 15, India), the East-Asian monastic order (Bloc 7).
- **The fractured remainder (Blocs 2,4,6,8,10,12,14,18) — Tier 2–4, mostly Weak · 0 to −1.** Leagues, nomad clans, sundered kingdoms, refugee flotillas, discordant unions — neutral or sliding hostile, peelable only faction by faction.

### Human factions
- **The Redoubt Authority — Tier 4, Strong** *(the PC's likely eventual home)* — the emergency government of the interior line: military remnants, organized empowered, and the Compact alliance. Registers and conscripts the empowered; rations; holds the line. *Internal tension: autonomy vs. conscription; fairness of the empowered-class system.*
- **The other redoubts — Tier 4** — Northern Europe, India: allied peers, barely in contact (comms dark).
- **Fractured humanity — Tier 1–3, Weak** — holdout enclaves, town militias, mutual-aid networks, and empowered strongmen. The default condition outside the redoubts.

### Monster-powers
- **The Feral Swarm — Tier 5, diffuse (no will)** — the universal ambient threat, marine-heavy; cannot be negotiated with, only survived. The reason coasts are death.
- **Sapient monster-lords — Tier 2–4 each, named** — rare kaiju-class beasts (the 1,000× tail) that break a district. Standalone threats (see bestiary).

## WORLD CLOCKS (the living year)
Each advances on its own; **player action can speed, slow, or reverse it.** Live values begin at the **Day-Zero baseline in file 07** and are tracked in the working state; the descriptions below give each clock's range and triggers.

- **Empire Consolidates the Heartland** — 8 seg. Advances as the Empire digests Eurasia; at 8/8 it turns its full weight outward toward the redoubts (the Year-Two threat). *Day Zero: 0/8.*
- **The Famine Wave** — 6 seg. Builds to a peak around Day 60 as trade severs, then ebbs where redoubts restore local food. *Day Zero: 0/6.*
- **The Concord Swing** — 6-seg two-way clock. Toward +: humanity proves it can hold → the Concord commits (a vast ally). Toward −: visible collapse → it defects. **The most consequential clock the PC can move.** *Day Zero: 3/6 (neutral).*
- **The Reaver Tide** — 4 seg. Presses south through the Mid-Atlantic; at 4/4 the corridor is fully lost and the interior line is directly threatened. *Day Zero: 0/4.*
- **Redoubt Recovery** — 8 seg. Power, water, order, and the rebuilt internal network; each segment opens new capacity. *Day Zero: 0/8.*
- **The Unmaking Horizon** — 12 seg, measured in years. The off-screen broader threat; mostly dread in Year One, but its slow tick is the campaign's doom-engine. *Day Zero: 0/12.*
