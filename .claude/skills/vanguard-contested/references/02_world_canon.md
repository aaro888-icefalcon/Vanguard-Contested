# 02 · WORLD CANON — Vanguard Contested, Year One
*The simulation's validated output, fixed as setting truth. Casualty figures are scenario illustrations; ranges, not predictions.*

## The premise (locked)
A mid-October Tuesday, ~14:00 UTC: the **Pulse**. One in five humans manifests a power; animals begin turning to monsters (most new biomass marine); rifts open; and an **EMP** kills unshielded electronics worldwide. Within **one week**, ~11 billion humanoid **crossers** arrive — eighteen refugee-nations fleeing a worse threat on their side. Seventeen are internally divided peoples whose stance is the *average* of their members; the eighteenth is the unified, aggressive **Empire**. In *Vanguard Contested*, the hostiles coordinate behind the Empire and humanity is beaten in the open — but a fighting alliance holds and a defended **redoubt** survives. Organized defeat, with somewhere to stand.

## Headline outcome (EMP-corrected)
- **First-year human dead: ~1.45 billion (≈18%).** Conflict ~1.12B · famine/systemic ~0.21B · Day-Zero pulse ~13M · transit losses ~0.11B.
- **~977M displaced-survivors** flee into the redoubts — so overrun regions empty through *both* death and mass flight.
- Casualty rate is ceiling-capped near ~19% (the 2%/month limit); the rest of a region's population loss is displacement.

## The map at year's end
**Redoubt (held, 8–15% casualty):** the North American interior (Interior North ~8%, the safest large zone on Earth), Interior South, Mountain West, Canada Core; Northern Europe (~13%); South Asia / India (~15%, held but bloodied — the largest population anchor). These are where humanity governs, rebuilds power and water in patches, and shelters the refugees.

**Contested (~16–17%):** Western Europe, the North American Southeast, Mexico & Central America, southern South America — defended in places, bleeding in others.

**Overrun / hostile-held (~16–19% dead, plus heavy flight):** the Empire's Eurasian heartland (Central Asia–Siberia, Russia); the Middle East (Warrior-Kingdom); New England and the Mid-Atlantic corridor (Reavers); Japan & Korea and the Pacific rim (corsairs + defected Houses); East Asia / China (squeezed); Southern and Eastern Europe; most of Africa; Oceania; northern South America. These end the year under hostile dominance, depopulated, or both.

## The year, in four movements
1. **The Day (hours).** Pulse and EMP together; aviation, critical care, and traffic take catastrophic losses; the world goes dark in an afternoon. No one understands what has happened.
2. **The First Two Weeks.** Rifts disgorge. The Empire lands its land vanguard across Central Asia–Siberia and moves with purpose while everyone else is blind; the Reavers come through into New England. Humanity, dark and ungoverned, cannot man fronts it cannot see; the Atlantic Concord hesitates.
3. **The First 90 Days.** The hostile coalition coheres around the Empire faster than a fractured humanity can answer. The Mid-Atlantic corridor is given up; the great civilian event of the season is the **evacuation west**. The interior line forms; Northern Europe and India harden around their loyal allies.
4. **The First Year.** The redoubt stabilizes (~2B behind a secure line); the Empire becomes the ascendant pole on Earth; the off-screen broader threat remains unanswered.

## The four driving questions, answered
- **New York City:** evacuated and largely lost as a unified city; the Mid-Atlantic corridor becomes contested no-man's-land. Holdout enclaves remain; governance relocates inland.
- **The Euro:** collapses. Markets froze at the EMP; credit and currency failed where states failed; barter and scrip fill the gap. The monetary union does not survive the first quarter.
- **The interior child (slice-of-life lens):** a guarded redoubt childhood — relocation inland, ration lines, wood-stove winters, shelter drills, a reconstituted school in shifts, a humanoid crosser family resettled a few towns over. Scarcity, not graphic peril.
- **Conflict map:** Empire-ascendant. The Empire holds the Eurasian heartland; the hostile core (Warrior-Kingdom, Corsair Order, Reavers, and the defected Pacific Houses) coordinates around it; humanity holds the redoubt behind a shrinking but real perimeter, with the loyal positive blocs at its side.

## The broader threat (the long horizon)
The crossers fled something. Canon shape: **the Unmaking** — a slow, world-consuming force or predator-power that renders planes uninhabitable behind it, which is why eighteen nations chose the desperate gamble of the rifts. Its clock is **slow (measured in years)**, so in Year One it is rumor, dread, and the reason the crossers are sympathetic rather than mere invaders — not an active force. It connects to the transcendents' planar cosmology and is the campaign's true endgame. Whether the rifts close, stay open, or become a door it can follow through is the central long-arc mystery. *(Definable further when you want the metaplot deepened.)*

## Standing content rules
History-book register; scale through consequence, never gore. Public roles by office, never named real individuals. Nuclear forces reach alert and signaling only — no detonation. No operational or weapons detail. The interior-child lens stays slice-of-life. Every figure is fictional.
